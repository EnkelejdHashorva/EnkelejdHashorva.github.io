About

I am Full Professor of Actuarial Science at HEC Lausanne, University of Lausanne.

My research focuses on stochastic analysis and applied probability, particularly rare events, extremes of random fields, regular variation, shift-invariant structures, multivariate dependence, and representations of asymptotic constants. Drawing on my actuarial expertise and industry experience at Allianz Suisse, I also work on applied problems in non-life insurance, risk management, and financial mathematics.

Together with Krzysztof Dębicki and his team, I have developed a theory of rare events for vector-valued Gaussian random fields, including the asymptotic constants governing their extremal behaviour.

I serve on the editorial boards of several international scientific journals, including the Journal of Applied Probability, Advances in Applied Probability, Probability and Mathematical Statistics, Statistics & Probability Letters, and the Spanish Journal of Statistics.

Before joining the University of Lausanne, I led the non-life insurance pricing team at Allianz Suisse Insurance. I have been a qualified actuary of the Swiss Association of Actuaries (SAA) since 2004 and have served on its Education Committee since 2011.

I completed my PhD at the University of Bern in 1999 under the supervision of Professor Jürg Hüsler, received the Venia Docendi in Applied Stochastics from the University of Bern in 2004, and hold the title of Privatdozent.

02

Research

What I am working on now, with the preprints and live illustrations that go with each theme.

Extremes of Gaussian random fields

The tail behaviour of suprema, infima and sojourn times of Gaussian processes and fields, scalar and vector-valued: how a rough field exceeds a high level, how long it stays there, and the Pickands–Piterbarg-type constants that quantify it. Recent work extends this to vector-valued stationary fields and to the much harder question of infima — a field staying high everywhere on a set.

Sojourns of vector-valued fields · Infima of Gaussian processes · illustration: Euler characteristic vs Pickands · illustration: staying high everywhere

Shift-invariant and max-stable structures

Stationarity in unusual places: max-stable fields indexed by rooted trees that look the same from every branch, Poisson particle systems that are stationary although each particle is anchored at its own origin, and the persistence of Brown–Resnick processes. The common thread is a representation of the asymptotic constants through shift-invariant measures.

Max-stable fields on rooted trees · Stationary Poisson particle systems · illustration: a max-stable field on a binary tree · illustration: a particle system with no clock of its own

Ruin and rare events for Brownian risk models

Finite-horizon ruin in forms that matter for reporting and regulation — ruin in every block or every sliding window, simultaneous ruin of several lines of business on a metric tree, Parisian ruin of branching populations — and Brownian motion with exponential resetting, where restarts make the search for a high level efficient. In each case the rare event is driven by a single mechanism that the asymptotics make explicit.

Block and sliding-window ruin · Ruin on Brownian metric trees · Parisian ruin of branching Brownian motion · Brownian motion with resetting · illustration: ruin in every block · illustration: back to square one

Laplace principles and exponential functionals

Tail and density asymptotics for homogeneous functionals of Gaussian vectors and for exponential integrals of Gaussian processes, via a radial–angular Laplace principle: the rare event chooses a direction, and the price of the constraint is read off from the geometry of a switching curve or an RKHS minimiser. Closely related: the long memory of Gaussian queues, whose workload covariance decays like a power of the lag.

Radial–angular Laplace principle · Gaussian exponential integrals · Covariance decay for Gaussian queues · illustration: the angle a rare event chooses · illustration: the most likely path

Gallery

Interactive illustrations of objects from recent papers. Everything is simulated live in your browser — move the sliders. Four of them below; the full gallery has all fifteen.

Back to square one

Animated: Brownian motion sent back to xR at Poisson times. The search for a level u has a best reset rate λ*, the steady state is an asymmetric Laplace law, and the tail of the supremum depends on the sign of xR — never reset, or reset exactly once, immediately. From Dębicki, Hashorva & Michna.

Ruin in every block, ruin in every window

Animated: Brownian paths rain down until one exceeds u in each of m reporting blocks, or in every sliding window of length h. The first hit crowds against h, and what follows is a finite-level event: p ∼ 2GmΨ((u+ch)/√h). From Dębicki, Hashorva & Michna.

A max-stable field on a binary tree

Brown–Resnick construction driven by a Gaussian branching random walk. Colours show which Poisson point wins at each vertex; zoom into any subtree and it looks like the whole.

Where the Euler characteristic ends and Pickands begins

Turn the roughness α of a Gaussian field from 2 downwards: the Euler-characteristic heuristic gives way to Pickands–Piterbarg asymptotics and the constant ℋα. A companion to Pavel Ievlev's illustration.
