Research & background

My research focuses on stochastic analysis and applied probability, particularly rare events, extremes of random fields, regular variation, shift-invariant structures, multivariate dependence, and representations of asymptotic constants. Drawing on my actuarial expertise and industry experience at Allianz Suisse, I also work on applied problems in non-life insurance, risk management, market management and financial mathematics.

Together with Krzysztof Dębicki and our teams, I have developed a theory of rare events for vector-valued Gaussian random fields, including the asymptotic constants governing their extremal behaviour.

Before joining the University of Lausanne, I led the non-life insurance pricing team at Allianz Suisse. I have been a qualified actuary of the Swiss Association of Actuaries (SAA) since 2004 and have served on its Education Committee since 2011.

I completed my PhD at the University of Bern in 1999 under the supervision of Professor Jürg Hüsler, received the Venia Docendi in Applied Stochastics from the University of Bern in 2004, and hold the title of Privatdozent.

Probability, illustrated

Explore objects from recent papers: move a slider, resample a path, and see what changes. These four illustrations are a starting point; the gallery has all seventeen.

Back to square one

Follow a drifting Brownian path between Poisson reset times. A reset can jump across the target. Compare first passage with the mean search time, and explore the asymmetric Laplace stationary law. From Dębicki, Hashorva & Michna.

Ruin in every block, ruin in every window

Compare crossing a level in each reporting block with crossing it in every sliding window. Brownian-bridge sampling detects within-block crossings; the sliding-window panel reports lower and upper bounds. From Dębicki, Hashorva & Michna.

A max-stable field on a binary tree

Construct a max-stable field from a Gaussian branching random walk. Colours identify the winning Poisson atom at each vertex. Increase the number of retained atoms to see how the finite approximation changes on the same realization. From Hashorva & Novikov.

Where the Euler characteristic ends and Pickands begins

Change the roughness of a Gaussian field and inspect its excursion set on a finite grid. The smooth case admits an Euler-characteristic comparison. For rough fields, the theoretical high-level tail involves the Pickands constant. A companion to Pavel Ievlev’s illustration.
