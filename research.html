Research

What I am working on now, with the preprints and live illustrations that go with each theme.

Extremes of Gaussian random fields

The tail behaviour of suprema, infima and sojourn times of Gaussian processes and fields, scalar and vector-valued: how a rough field exceeds a high level, how long it stays there, and the Pickands–Piterbarg-type constants that quantify it. Recent work extends this to vector-valued stationary fields and to the much harder question of infima — a field staying high everywhere on a set.

Sojourns of vector-valued fields · Infima of Gaussian processes · illustration: Euler characteristic vs Pickands · illustration: staying high everywhere

Shift-invariant and max-stable structures

Stationarity in unusual places: max-stable fields indexed by rooted trees that look the same from every branch, Poisson particle systems that are stationary although each particle is anchored at its own origin, and the persistence of Brown–Resnick processes. The common thread is a representation of the asymptotic constants through shift-invariant measures.

Max-stable fields on rooted trees · Stationary Poisson particle systems · illustration: a max-stable field on a binary tree · illustration: a particle system with no clock of its own

Ruin and rare events for Brownian risk models

Finite-horizon ruin in forms that matter for reporting and regulation — ruin in every block or every sliding window, simultaneous ruin of several lines of business on a metric tree, Parisian ruin of branching populations — and Brownian motion with exponential resetting, where restarts make the search for a high level efficient. In each case the rare event is driven by a single mechanism that the asymptotics make explicit.

Block and sliding-window ruin · Ruin on Brownian metric trees · Parisian ruin of branching Brownian motion · Brownian motion with resetting · illustration: ruin in every block · illustration: back to square one

Laplace principles and exponential functionals

Tail and density asymptotics for homogeneous functionals of Gaussian vectors and for exponential integrals of Gaussian processes, via a radial–angular Laplace principle: the rare event chooses a direction, and the price of the constraint is read off from the geometry of a switching curve or an RKHS minimiser. Closely related: the long memory of Gaussian queues, whose workload covariance decays like a power of the lag.
