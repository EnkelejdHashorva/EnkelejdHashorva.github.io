Parisian ruin of branching Brownian motion

One Brownian particle starts at the origin; at rate β a particle splits into k children whose motions may be correlated. Against the rising line u + ct, simultaneous Parisian ruin means: at some moment, every particle alive stays above the line for a whole window of length h — the lower envelope Y(t) = minv Xv(t) − ct exceeds u throughout the window. The probability is C e−κu with κ = c + √(c² + 2β): the rate depends only on the slope and the splitting rate, while the offspring numbers, correlations, window and observation grid all sit in the constant. Conditional on ruin, the window starts around time u/a, a = √(c² + 2β), with Gaussian spread √(u/a³). The animation replays trees found by brute force that achieve ruin. From Hashorva, Novikov & Shashkov, Parisian ruin of branching Brownian motion.

Euler step 0.01, horizon 3u/a + 3, population capped at 300; up to 4 000 attempts per search. The theorem holds for prescribed offspring and covariance sequences; here every split uses the same k and the same equicorrelation ρ (valid for ρ ≥ −1/(k−1)).
