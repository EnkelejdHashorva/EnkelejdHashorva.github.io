A particle system with no clock of its own

Countably many particles follow Fj(t) = Rj ⊙ Z(j)(t): independent copies of a single mark process Z, each rescaled componentwise by an atom Rj of a Poisson measure with the scale-invariant intensity ∏ ri−2dri. Taking logarithms turns scaling into shifting, so the picture is a cloud of copies of log Z displaced by Poisson points of intensity e−xdx. The paper's point: the system can be stationary although the mark is not, and stationarity is a property of the intensity νZ, not of Z. The mixture surprise

A finite mixture of marks with shift-invariant intensities has a shift-invariant intensity. The converse is the paper's question, and for log-Gaussian marks on a connected time set the answer is yes: every component must itself be stationary. Drop connectedness and it fails. Example 4.2 takes T = ℤ/2ℤ, so a trajectory is just a pair (F(0), F(1)), and stationarity means this cloud is symmetric about the diagonal. With log Zk(1) ∼ N(0,2) and N(−2,2) neither component is — their two level counts differ by a factor e — and yet the mixture {ℒ(Z1) + e ℒ(Z2)}/(1+e) is exactly symmetric.
