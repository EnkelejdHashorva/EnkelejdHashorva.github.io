Persistence of a Brown–Resnick process

A stationary Brown–Resnick process is the upper envelope X(t) = supi Pi Z(i)(t) of countably many spectral curves, each a log-normal Z(t) = exp(√2 BH(t) − t2H) scaled by a point Pi of a Poisson process. Watch the construction assemble on the left. Two very different questions about a level u: how many curves exceed it somewhere on [0,T] — Poisson with mean ℋV(T)/u, the Pickands functional, growing linearly in T — and how many stay above it throughout — Poisson with mean 𝒢V(T)/u, the expected infimum E inf[0,T] Z, which decays like 2Ψ(√γV(T)) for H > ½ and with an extra factor T−1 for Brownian motion. Several curves can together keep X above u without any one of them doing so — the picture makes that plain. From Dębicki, Hashorva & Novikov, Expected infimum and persistence probabilities of log-normal stationary Brown–Resnick processes.
