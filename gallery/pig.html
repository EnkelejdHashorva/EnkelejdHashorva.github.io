Similar claim counts, different premiums

Given a hidden frailty Θ with mean 1 and variance 1/τ, a policy's number of claims in one period is Poisson with mean μΘ. A gamma frailty makes the count negative binomial (NB); an inverse Gaussian frailty makes it Poisson–inverse Gaussian (PIG). For the same μ and τ the two counts share their mean and variance, and at low claim frequencies their probabilities are almost identical: both distances below are of third order in μ. But if the frailty persists from one period to the next, the premium after a claim history follows the posterior mean E[Θ | N = k], the renewal factor. After two claims it involves the third moment of Θ, the first one the two frailties do not share; as μ → 0, the two renewal factors after two claims differ by about 8–27% for τ between ½ and 2. In the paper, each leaf (terminal risk group) of a Bayesian CART tree carries such a law; this page looks at a single leaf. From Hashorva & Murillo Hernández, Poisson–inverse Gaussian leaves for Bayesian CART claims frequency models.

All numbers are exact for the two fixed laws; nothing is simulated or fitted. Probabilities use the negative binomial recurrence and a three-term recurrence for the PIG law, summed up to 400 claims. The renewal factor is E[Θ | N = k] = (k + 1) pk+1 / (μ pk); the distances are dTV = ½ Σ |pk − qk| and H² = ½ Σ (√pk − √qk)². For μ = 0.01, 0.05, 0.1 and τ = ½, 1, 2, the readouts reproduce the paper's direct comparison table. The paper's expansions are for low claim frequencies; at larger μ the comparison can change, even in sign: try μ = 0.5 with τ = ¼.
