The angle a rare event chooses

Let B be planar Brownian motion and Y(r) = ‖B(r)‖². For 0 < s < t ask for both Y(s) > u and Y(t) > bu at once. Writing the driving Gaussian vector in ℝ⁴ as Z = RU with R² ∼ χ²₄ and U uniform on the sphere, both constraints become a single radial one, R > √u · q(U), where the cost q = max(q₁, q₂) is the larger of the two required radii. The exponential rate is ½ min q²; the prefactor is decided by the local geometry where the minimum is attained. Move b and watch the conditional angular cloud pass through the three mechanisms: a slack constraint leaves a full Gaussian blob, a weakly contacting one asymptotically cuts it in half, and a genuinely switching pair moves the minimiser off and costs a factor u−1/2. From E. Hashorva, A radial–angular Laplace principle for homogeneous functionals (Theorem C.1).

The radial–angular identity pb(u) = E[ F̄χ²₄(u q(U)²) ] is exact, and for four degrees of freedom F̄(x) = (1 + x/2)e−x/2. The displayed Monte Carlo values average this function over 40 000 uniform directions. This avoids counting rare hits, but still has sampling error, especially when the angular mass is concentrated. The effective sample size (ESS) and estimated relative standard error are shown below the estimate. The cloud resamples these finitely many directions with weight F̄(u q(U)²), approximating the exact conditional angular law. Half-clouds and Gaussian shapes describe the large-u limit, not exact finite-u shapes.
