The angle a rare event chooses

Let B be planar Brownian motion and Y(r) = ‖B(r)‖². For 0 < s < t ask for both Y(s) > u and Y(t) > bu at once. Writing the driving Gaussian vector in ℝ⁴ as Z = RU with R² ∼ χ²₄ and U uniform on the sphere, both constraints become a single radial one, R > √u · q(U), where the cost q = max(q₁, q₂) is the larger of the two required radii. The exponential rate is min q²; the prefactor is decided by the local geometry where the minimum is attained. Move b and watch the conditional angular cloud pass through the three mechanisms: a slack constraint leaves a full Gaussian blob, a weakly contacting one cuts it exactly in half, and a genuinely switching pair moves the minimiser off and costs a factor u−1/2. From E. Hashorva, A radial–angular Laplace principle for homogeneous functionals (Theorem C.1).

The radial–angular identity pb(u) = E[ F̄χ²₄(u q(U)²) ] is exact, and for four degrees of freedom F̄(x) = (1 + x/2)e−x/2. So every number here comes from averaging an explicit function over 40 000 uniform directions — no rare-event simulation, valid at any u. The cloud is those same directions resampled with weight F̄(u q(U)²), which is exactly the conditional angular law.
