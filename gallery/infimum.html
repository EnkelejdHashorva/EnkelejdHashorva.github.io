Staying high everywhere: the infimum of fractional Brownian motion

How does a fractional Brownian motion manage to stay above a high level u on a whole set 𝒦 = [a₁,b₁] ∪ [a₂,b₂]? The exponential rate is set by an optimal measure μ* minimising Var ∫X dμ, and its potential m(t) = Cov(X(t), Xμ*)/Var Xμ* ≥ 1; the contact set {m = 1} decides the prefactor. For H > ½ the measure is the point mass at a₁, the contact set is that single point, and the conditioned paths touch u there and lift off — dominated contact, with P ~ P(BH(a₁) > u). For Brownian motion the potential is ≡ 1 on all of 𝒦: full contact, the paths hug the level across both intervals, and a Laplace-transform reduction gives the extra factor κ a₁3/2/√(2π) · u−2·u relative to the one-point tail. From Dębicki, Hashorva & Novikov, Gumbel MDA & tail behaviour of infima of Gaussian processes.
