How long does a Gaussian queue remember?

A fluid queue is fed by fractional Brownian motion and drained at rate c: the stationary workload is Q(t) = supu≥0 (W(t) − W(t−u)) with W(t) = BH(t) − ct. The marginal law of Q has been understood for decades; its temporal dependence — an open problem listed by Dębicki & Mandjes — is what this paper settles. The lookback time τ, how far into the past the supremum reaches, is the key: for H ≠ ½, Cov(Q(0), Q(r)) ~ H(2H−1) (E τ)2 r2H−2 — the queue inherits the dependence range of its input, positive and non-integrable for H > ½, eventually negative for H < ½. Brownian input (H = ½) is the Markovian exception, with exponential decay r−3/2 e−c²r/2 (Abate–Whitt). From Dębicki, Hashorva & Novikov, Covariance decay for Gaussian queues.

Fractional Gaussian noise by circulant embedding (Davies–Harte, FFT), time step 0.02, eight independent runs of length 5 243 each, discarding the first 15% as burn-in. The running-minimum construction starts at time zero, so this is a finite-history approximation to the stationary queue. Differences between independent runs estimate covariance without the downward bias from subtracting each run’s own sample mean. The discrete supremum underestimates the continuous one; finite history and long memory can cause further bias, especially for high H and small c. Large-lag dots are noisy, and the dashed fBm constant is itself estimated.
