Hitting a quadrant: the correlated Brownian motions that make it

Two Brownian motions with correlation ρ, each with drift −1, start at the origin and, almost surely, drift off to −∞. Rarely, at some time both are above their thresholds α₁u and α₂u at once: the path enters the shaded quadrant. The animation shows only those rare paths. Watch where and when they enter: for weak correlation both coordinates have to work and the entry happens near the corner at time ≈ t₀u; for correlation above ρ* = (α₁+α₂)/(2α₁) the second coordinate comes along for free, the problem collapses to one dimension and the entry drifts up the vertical edge at time ≈ α₁u. From Dębicki, Hashorva, Ji & Rolski, Extremal behaviour of hitting a cone by correlated Brownian motion with drift.

Paths are found by brute force (up to 60 000 attempts, Euler step 0.01), so large u with weak correlation may return only a few. The theory predicts P(u) ≈ C uκ e−ĝu/2 with ĝ = 2(α₁+α₂+2t₀)/(1+ρ) in regime 1 and ĝ = 4α₁ in regime 2, and Gaussian fluctuations of the entry time around its centre.
