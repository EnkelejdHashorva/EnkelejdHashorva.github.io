Ruin in every block, ruin in every window

The loss process X(t) = B(t) − ct of a Brownian risk model is watched over a horizon T = mh cut into m reporting blocks of length h. Block ruin asks for an exceedance of the level u in every block; sliding-window ruin asks for one in every window of length h, wherever it is placed — equivalently, no recovery interval of length h. Both probabilities are bounded by first-block ruin and hence by ordinary ruin on [0, T]; they are not products of one-block probabilities. As u → ∞ with h, c, m fixed, conditional on ruin in the first block, the first hit of u concentrates just before h; the strong Markov property there turns everything that follows into a finite-level event, so pbl ∼ 2 Gm Ψ((u+ch)/√h) and psl ∼ 2 Gslm Ψ((u+ch)/√h), where Gm is the probability that a fresh drifted Brownian motion is positive somewhere in each of the blocks [kh,(k+1)h], k = 1,…,m−2, and Gslm the Parisian-type survival probability that no length-h interval within [0,(m−1)h] is entirely non-positive. The animation rains down Brownian paths until one achieves the event, then replays it. From Dębicki, Hashorva & Michna, Finite-horizon block and sliding-window ruin for Brownian risk models.

Brownian increments are sampled at step h/200 and joined by straight lines. Block maxima and recovery intervals are evaluated on this polygonal path. The constants Gm, Gslm and Jm = P{B(jh) − cjh > 0, 1 ≤ j ≤ m−2} use 4 000 such paths; for m = 2 their exact values are 1. The estimates can be noisy or zero. The theoretical ordering is Jm ≤ Gslm ≤ Gm ≤ 1. Interpolation misses Brownian fluctuations between samples; the sliding-event error has no asserted direction. A fixed mesh does not validate a high-level limit, and 2GΨ is an asymptotic, not a finite-level identity. Wilson bars are the usual fixed-sample approximation for sampling uncertainty; they do not account for numerical error or provide a sequential confidence guarantee. Brute-force search becomes slow when first-block ruin is rare.
