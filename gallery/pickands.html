Where the Euler characteristic ends and Pickands begins

A stationary Gaussian field on the unit square with covariance r(t) = exp(−(|t₁|α + |t₂|α)/ℓα), so that r(t) ≈ 1 − (|t₁|α+|t₂|α)/ℓα near the origin. For α = 2 the field is smooth and the excursion set above a high level is a few clean islands: the Euler-characteristic heuristic of Adler–Taylor (the one in Pavel Ievlev's illustration) gives P(sup X > u) ≈ E χ(Au). For α < 2 the field is not differentiable, the excursion set turns to dust, E χ is infinite, and the tail is governed instead by the Pickands–Piterbarg asymptotics P(sup X > u) ~ ℋα2 ℓ−2 u4/α Ψ(u), with the Pickands constant ℋα — known in closed form only for α = 1 (ℋ₁ = 1) and α = 2 (ℋ₂ = 1/√π).

The grid supremum underestimates the true one, more so for small α — one reason the “implied” constant drifts. The implied constant is simply √( PMC / (ℓ−2 u4/α Ψ(u)) ): what the simulation would need ℋα to be. It is a finite-u caricature, not an estimator.

The shape of the field near a high maximum

One-dimensional slice. Conditioning on X(0) = u and zooming in by u−2/α in time and u in height, the paths converge to √2 Bα(s) − |s|α, where Bα is fractional Brownian motion. For α = 2 this is a parabola with a random tilt; for smaller α it is a rough random cusp. This is the picture behind Pickands' double-sum method.
