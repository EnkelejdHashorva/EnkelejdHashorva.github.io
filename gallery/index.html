Insurance models

Near misses that add up

Explore a two-group lognormal example in which both sums cross a high threshold but one group maximum can fall short. The shaded region accounts for the difference. At unit spread the asymptotic sum-to-maximum probability ratio is about 1.1821. From Hashorva.

Similar claim counts, different premiums

Compare Poisson–inverse Gaussian and negative binomial counts with matched means and variances. The claim-count probabilities approach each other at low frequency, while the renewal factors after two claims can still differ when the same frailty persists into the next period. From Hashorva & Murillo Hernández.

Passage, ruin and queues

Back to square one

Follow a drifting Brownian path between Poisson reset times. A reset can jump across the target. Compare first passage with the mean search time, and explore the asymmetric Laplace stationary law. From Dębicki, Hashorva & Michna.

Hitting a quadrant

Watch correlated Brownian paths with drift reach two barriers. Vary the correlation through its critical value to see how the most likely entry moves between the corner and an edge. Paths and hits are observed on a finite time grid. From Dębicki, Hashorva, Ji & Rolski.

Simultaneous exceedance, stationary case

For a bivariate stationary Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process, compare the first detected entry times with their high-level uniform limit. Vary the correlation to see when the second barrier still affects entry. From Dębicki, Hashorva & Wang.

How long does a Gaussian queue remember?

Compare simulated workload covariances with their predicted decay as the lag increases. At H = ½ the exact finite-lag Brownian covariance provides a benchmark. From Dębicki, Hashorva & Novikov.

Parisian ruin of branching Brownian motion

Follow the minimum position across the living particles in a branching Brownian tree. The simulation checks a Parisian window at sampled times, and the paper also treats continuous observation. From Hashorva, Popivoda, Novikov & Shashkov.

Ruin on a Brownian metric tree

Follow the leaf barriers and the currents in a Brownian metric tree. A binding barrier can carry zero current. The presets show how to distinguish that case from a strictly slack leaf. From Hashorva.

Ruin in every block, ruin in every window

Compare crossing a level in each reporting block with crossing it in every sliding window. Brownian-bridge sampling detects within-block crossings; the sliding-window panel reports lower and upper bounds. From Dębicki, Hashorva & Michna.

Gaussian excursions and integrals

Where the Euler characteristic ends and Pickands begins

Change the roughness of a Gaussian field and inspect its excursion set on a finite grid. The smooth case admits an Euler-characteristic comparison. For rough fields, the theoretical high-level tail involves the Pickands constant. A companion to Pavel Ievlev’s illustration.

The most likely path of a large exponential integral

Compare fractional Brownian paths with a large exponential integral against a numerical minimum-norm path. At H = ½, an exact continuous-time calculation benchmarks the discretisation. From Hashorva & Novikov.

Staying high everywhere

How does fractional Brownian motion stay high on two disjoint intervals? For H > ½ the optimal potential touches the level at the first endpoint. For Brownian motion the entire set is in contact. Compare the simulated paths with these profiles. From Dębicki, Hashorva & Novikov.

Sojourns of a bivariate Gaussian field

Inspect two correlated Gaussian fields, their joint excursion set and its grid area. Change the level, correlation or correlation length and compare the sampled area with its pointwise-probability benchmark. From Dębicki, Hashorva & Michna.

The angle a rare event chooses

Vary the ratio of two thresholds on a squared Brownian norm. Watch the conditional angular cloud approach a full Gaussian profile, a half-space profile at a transition, or a switching boundary. From Hashorva.

Max-stable processes and particle systems

A max-stable field on a binary tree

Construct a max-stable field from a Gaussian branching random walk. Colours identify the winning Poisson atom at each vertex. Increase the number of retained atoms to see how the finite approximation changes on the same realization. From Hashorva & Novikov.

Persistence of a Brown–Resnick process

Build a Brown–Resnick envelope from a finite collection of spectral curves. Compare crossing a level somewhere with staying above it everywhere. Different curves can take turns supporting the envelope. From Dębicki, Hashorva & Novikov.

A particle system with no clock of its own

Poisson-rescaled copies of a mark can form a stationary particle system even when the individual mark is non-stationary. Compare two phase laws and a Brown–Resnick mark, then explore a two-time mixture. From Hashorva.
