The most likely path of a large exponential integral

Let X be a fractional Brownian motion with Hurst index H on [0,1] and I = ∫₀¹ eX(t) dt. How does I become larger than a big b? The answer is a variational problem in the reproducing kernel Hilbert space: the cheapest path hb with ∫ehb ≥ b, whose norm ρb gives the Jensen–Gaussian lower bound P(I > b) ≥ Ψ(ρb). The variational path concentrates its exponential mass near the variance-maximising endpoint t = 1; below we compare a numerical candidate with simulated exceedances. The bound is asymptotically exact when H > ½; for Brownian motion the ratio tends to √2; for rougher paths the cited result gives logarithmic asymptotics, without an exact ratio limit. From Hashorva & Novikov, Tail and density asymptotics for Gaussian exponential integrals.
